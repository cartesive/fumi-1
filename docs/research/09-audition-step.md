# 09 — The audition step

Written 9 Oct 2026 after your first listening test. This becomes its own milestone, **M2.5 "Audition"**, between the pitch core (M2) and building the koto voice (M3). Nothing about the koto engine is decided before it.

## What your ears found

- The DX7 **KOTO** patch is close to the Suiko koto but **too thin**.
- **HARP 2** is closer, and has a clear **click** at the start.
- Hunches to test: a filter taming the twang, a touch of subtle reverb, and the tuning itself giving a "resonance".

What that tells us already:

- The ST-50's koto is in **FM-pluck territory**, not physical-model territory. A harp patch being the nearest match says its body is rounder and fuller than a real koto's, with a distinct attack transient on top. That fits a 1980s instrument and it fits the sellers' "warm" descriptions.
- The click matters. It is probably a separate short component (in FM terms, a fast-decaying high-ratio modulator or its own operator pair), not a by-product of the main tone. That means it can be dialled independently.
- So the starting point for FuMi's koto is a **6-operator FM patch**, and the string model drops to second candidate. FuMi can carry Felucca's FM6 engine (the same Dexed code the DX7 patches run on), so anything auditioned as a DX7 patch transfers exactly.

## On the three hunches

**Tuning as resonance: almost certainly real, and testable.** With pure fourths and fifths, the partials of notes ringing together line up instead of beating (the 3rd harmonic of the tonic sits exactly on the 2nd harmonic of the fifth). On an instrument where plucked notes overlap and ring into reverb, that reads as calm, glowing sustain; in equal temperament the same notes shimmer slightly out of focus. The narrow semitones add the plaintive colour. This is the cheapest beauty in the project, which is why the audition includes the same phrase in equal and Wa tuning.

**Subtle reverb: very plausible**, and it compounds the tuning effect by keeping earlier notes sounding. Whether the ST-50 has it built in or the recordings add it is unknown; audition dry against a short, dark, low-level reverb.

**The filter: worth pinning down which way.** "Twang" lives in the upper mids and highs, so taming it is a **high cut** (low-pass), or a dip around 2–4 kHz. A **low cut** (high-pass) would make a patch thinner, and thin is the complaint about KOTO. The two may both be present: a gentle low cut to keep the body from booming on a small speaker, and a high cut for the twang. The audition has both as separate controls so you can find it by ear rather than by name.

## Why "too thin", and how a hybrid fixes it

Without the patch data in front of me I cannot say which of these KOTO lacks, but thinness in an FM pluck is nearly always some of:

1. A single carrier doing all the work. Fix: a second carrier pair, slightly detuned (a few cents), for width and slow movement.
2. Too much modulation for too long: bright and wiry. Fix: lower modulator level, faster modulator decay, so the tone rounds off quickly after the attack.
3. No low partial. Fix: a quiet carrier an octave down, or a lower carrier ratio.
4. A decay that drops straight to nothing. Fix: two-stage envelope, quick fall then a long quiet tail.

A hybrid is natural in 6-operator FM because a patch is two or three independent stacks added together. Use an algorithm of three carrier-modulator pairs and give each a job:

| Stack | Job | Taken from |
|---|---|---|
| A | The click and bright first 30 ms | HARP 2's attack, or KOTO's if it is sharper |
| B | The body and the long decay | HARP 2's body |
| C | Fullness: a detuned double of B, or the octave below | New |

## How the audition runs

Two rigs, because they answer different questions.

### Rig 1: on the FM-1's own speaker, no custom code

The stock firmware (and FM-1+VA, and Felucca's FM6) loads standard DX7 32-voice banks. So the audition set is **a `.syx` bank of 32 koto candidates** generated by script: KOTO and HARP 2 unchanged as anchors, then hybrids and variations in labelled steps (click amount, brightness, detune, octave-down level, decay shape).

- Load it like any DX7 bank; play each on the real speaker, headphones and line out.
- Use the stock firmware's own reverb to try dry against wet.
- Limits: equal temperament only, piano key layout, no extra filter. This rig judges **timbre**.

### Rig 2: an audition page on the Mac

The host bench renders the same patches through the same FM engine, then adds what the stock firmware cannot: Wa tuning and depth, the low-cut and high-cut filters, the reverb, and the scale laid out as phrases. Output is one local HTML page of short clips with the reference alongside, level-matched:

- single notes low, middle, high;
- a slow in-scale phrase with notes ringing over each other (this is where tuning shows);
- a fast glissando;
- a press-bend.

Each clip row has the variants side by side and a place to mark a score. This rig judges **tuning, filter, reverb and phrasing**.

### The loop

1. Dump KOTO and HARP 2 to readable operator settings and diff them. (You provide the `.syx` bank you loaded; the FM-1's stock factory bank has only generic names, STRING 1 and so on, and does not contain these two.)
2. Generate round 1: about 30 candidates around both.
3. You listen on both rigs and rank. Top three go forward.
4. Round 2 varies around the winners in finer steps. Two or three rounds should converge.
5. The winner is written into FuMi as its own patch, in readable form, and locked with a golden-file test.

Then the same step repeats for shakuhachi, strings and the "Suiko" voice. Auditioning is the method for every voice, not a one-off.
